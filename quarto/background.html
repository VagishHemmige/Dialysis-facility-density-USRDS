Author
Affiliation

When to start dialysis

There is no formal eGFR threshold below which maintenance hemodialysis should begin. Most nephrologists use the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) as a major factor, together with clinical signs and symptoms, the availability of dialysis access, and patient preference (Hemmige et al. 2024). The IDEAL trial randomized adults with progressive chronic kidney disease to start dialysis at an estimated GFR of 10 to 14 mL/min (early) or 5 to 7 mL/min (late), and found no survival benefit from starting early (Cooper et al. 2010). After its publication in 2010, the trend toward earlier initiation reversed (Hemmige et al. 2024), which is why the cohort here begins in 2011.

Why facility density

If eGFR alone does not dictate when dialysis starts, the surrounding health system may. The hypothesis of the paper is that areas organized around high-intensity treatment of end-stage disease, with many dialysis facilities per resident, start dialysis earlier than areas oriented toward prevention (Hemmige et al. 2024). The unit is the Dartmouth Atlas Hospital Service Area (HSA): a group of ZIP codes whose residents get most of their hospital care locally, about 3,400 in the US (The Dartmouth Atlas of Health Care, n.d.-a). The paper calls these “health service areas”; this site keeps the abbreviation HSA.

The measures

  • eGFR at initiation is calculated by the USRDS from the serum creatinine reported on the CMS-2728 Medical Evidence form, using the CKD-EPI equation (Levey et al. 2009) with the race coefficient, because that is the value physicians saw when deciding. The paper’s Methods call it “CKD-EPI equation 2012”; see the differences page.
  • Facility density counts the dialysis facilities listed by CMS in the patient’s HSA on the date dialysis started, per 100,000 residents (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services 2025).
  • Area characteristics come from the American Community Survey (Walker and Herman, n.d.), the USDA rural-urban commuting area codes (USDA Economic Research Service 2020), and Dartmouth Atlas primary care and hospital measures (The Dartmouth Atlas of Health Care, n.d.-c, n.d.-b).

The eGFR thresholds

Three eGFR values, all in mL/min/1.73 m², shape the analysis:

  • 20, the cohort’s ceiling. Patients starting above it are excluded, because the paper restricts to the range nephrologists target for starting dialysis (Hemmige et al. 2024). As in the original code, 20 itself is kept.
  • 10, the definition of an early start. It is the lower edge of the IDEAL trial’s early-start range (Cooper et al. 2010). As in the original code, 10 itself counts as early.
  • 5, in one extra analysis. The original R code also fit the full model only among patients with eGFR above 5. The code does not record why. This analysis is not in the paper.

Race and eGFR

The race coefficient of the 2009 CKD-EPI equation raises the eGFR of a patient recorded as Black by about 16% for the same creatinine (Levey et al. 2009). A Black patient is therefore more likely than another patient with the same creatinine to be counted as starting at an eGFR of 10 or above. Black patients make up a larger share of the patients in denser HSAs (11.3% in HSAs with no facility and 40.6% in the densest, in the paper’s Table 1 (Hemmige et al. 2024)). The models adjust for race, and the paper also fits them separately for Black and White patients (eTable 1). Current practice uses the 2021 equation without race (Inker et al. 2021); the replication keeps the paper’s measure, because the question is how physicians acted on the eGFR they saw.
