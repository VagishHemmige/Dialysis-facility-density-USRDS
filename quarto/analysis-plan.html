Analysis plan: Replication of “Geographic Dialysis Facility Density and Early Dialysis Initiation”

Status: draft.

Scope and reporting framework

This document is the authoritative specification of the study’s scientific objectives, population, definitions, estimands and statistical methods. ARCHITECTURE.md documents the implementation, AGENTS.md the working conventions, and GitHub Issues the open decisions and implementation status. Neither file supersedes this plan.

This is a replication. The design is the one published in JAMA Network Open (Hemmige et al. 2024), and every choice the paper states is treated as decided and cited to it. The original analysis code (an R Markdown file from October 2023, kept in original/) is the second source: where it makes a choice the paper does not state, the plan adopts the code’s choice and says so. Where the code and the paper disagree, or the code contains an error, the point is marked Open and linked to a decision issue for the deciders. Each such point is also listed on the website’s “Differences from the original code” page.

Reporting will follow STROBE (Elm et al. 2007) and RECORD (Benchimol et al. 2015). The reporting map below links each checklist item to a section of this plan.

1. Objective

To test whether the density of dialysis facilities in a patient’s Hospital Service Area (HSA; the paper calls it a health service area) is associated with starting in-center hemodialysis early, defined as an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 10 mL/min/1.73 m² or above at initiation (section 7) (Hemmige et al. 2024).

  • Primary hypothesis (stated in advance by the paper): patients in HSAs with higher facility density have higher odds of initiating hemodialysis at eGFR > 10 mL/min/1.73 m².
  • Secondary: the same association with eGFR as a continuous outcome; with density as a continuous exposure; within strata of race (White, Black).
  • Exploratory: the share of variation in eGFR at initiation explained by HSA and by dialysis facility.
  • Replication objective: to reproduce the paper’s Figure 1 (cohort flow), Table 1, Table 2 and eTables 1 to 3 from public code, using usRds and flowchart, and to quantify the effect of each correction to the original code.

2. Design

Cross-sectional study of incident hemodialysis patients linked to area-level data (Hemmige et al. 2024). The question is associational; no causal estimand or target-trial emulation is used.

Secondary-data declaration. The investigators have analyzed this USRDS release before: it is the release used for the published paper, and the published results are known. Every result of this replication is therefore a reproduction, not a new test of the hypothesis.

Data currency. The facility snapshot current on each patient’s date of dialysis start is used; a snapshot describes facilities from its CMS release date until the day before the next release.

4. Study population

Source population: every patient with a CMS-2728 Medical Evidence form in the USRDS.

Index event: start of regular chronic dialysis (DIALDAT) on the patient’s first Medical Evidence form.

Eligibility, in order. Each step is one box of the cohort flowchart. Steps 1 to 9 come from the paper and the original code; steps 10 to 16 are linkage and completeness requirements from the original code.

  1. First Medical Evidence form per patient (earliest MEFDATE); later forms describe a second or later dialysis episode.
  2. Dialysis started (DIALDAT) 2011 to 2019, as the paper states. The paper starts in 2011 because of the IDEAL trial (Cooper et al. 2010). The original code used the year of the form (MEFDATE), applying the lower bound explicitly and the upper bound only implicitly, through the ACS linkage (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026).
  3. Age 18 or older at incidence (INC_AGE).
  4. Hemodialysis as the primary type of dialysis (DIALTYP = hemodialysis).
  5. In-center dialysis: DIALSET “Dialysis facility/center”. The paper excludes patients who began home dialysis. The original code kept codes 1 and 2, which excluded home dialysis in the coding of the time (Appendix C format $MESET_CUR: 1 hospital inpatient, 2 dialysis facility/center, 3 home; 14,380 patients excluded in the published Figure 1). The current coding (format $MESET, 2024 Researcher’s Guide) has 1 = home and 2 = dialysis facility/center, so the replication keeps code 2 only, the same intent in the current coding (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026).
  6. Insurance status recorded (at least one coverage field marked yes).
  7. Vascular access type at first outpatient dialysis recorded (ACCESSTYPE not unknown).
  8. Sex recorded.
  9. eGFR at initiation recorded and no higher than 20 mL/min/1.73 m², as in the original code (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026, in a working session: “Use the code”). The paper says “less than 20”, and its Figure 1 labels the 38,708 excluded patients “eGFR <20”. As in the original code, missing eGFR is removed in the same step.
  10. A residential ZIP code in effect on the date of dialysis start (RESIDENC period containing DIALDAT), unique per patient.
  11. The ZIP code has a ZCTA in the UDS Mapper crosswalk for the year dialysis started, and ACS data are available for that ZCTA and year. The original code intended this crosswalk but never applied it, matching ZIP codes to ZCTAs directly; the replication applies it (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026, in a working session: “I intended at some point to cross-walk this but never did”).
  12. A facility snapshot current on the date of dialysis start. The original code’s facility join also required the year of the form to match the year dialysis started; the replication drops that requirement (see “Year of linkage” below).
  13. An HSA for the ZIP code in the crosswalk for the year dialysis started.
  14. Residence in the 48 contiguous states or the District of Columbia (excludes Alaska, Hawaii, territories, and states not assigned a Census region).
  15. Race recorded (not unknown) and Hispanic ethnicity recorded (not unknown).
  16. ZIP code has a RUCA code other than 99 (zero-population ZIP code) and complete ACS covariates.

The original code applied several of these filters outside its CONSORT table, and its table misreported others; see section 12. In this replication every exclusion is a flowchart step, so the diagram reproduces the cohort exactly.

Year of linkage. All area data (ZCTA crosswalk, ACS, HSA, HSA population) are matched to the year dialysis started (DIALDAT), the same year as the facility counts. The original code matched area data to the year of the Medical Evidence form (MEFDATE) and its facility join silently required the two years to be equal; the replication corrects this (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026).

5. Exposure

HSA dialysis facility density: the number of dialysis facilities in the patient’s HSA on the date of dialysis start, divided by the HSA’s population, times 100,000 (Hemmige et al. 2024).

  • Facilities: every facility listed in the CMS release current on the date of dialysis start (usRds::add_dialysis_facility_counts()), assigned to an HSA by its ZIP code and the crosswalk for that calendar year. Facilities whose ZIP code has no HSA are not counted.
  • HSA population: the ACS total population of the ZCTAs of the ZIP codes in the HSA, using the Dartmouth and ZCTA crosswalks and ACS for the year dialysis started. Each ZCTA is counted once per HSA, because several ZIP codes can share a ZCTA. ZCTAs without ACS data contribute nothing.
  • Categories: 0 facilities; then cut at the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles of density among all patients in the final cohort (zeros included), each category including its upper bound. The paper reports cut points of 1.36, 1.95 and 2.76 per 100,000 (Hemmige et al. 2024).
  • Continuous version: density per 100,000, for sensitivity analyses.
  • Also described: facility stations per 100,000, and for-profit and non-profit facility counts.

6. Covariates

All patient covariates are measured on the first Medical Evidence form, at initiation. Area covariates are fixed for the patient’s ZIP code or HSA as described in section 3.

The five published counts sum to each column’s total, so the form’s Middle Eastern, South Asian and Other/multiracial codes were merged into those groups in the Stata analysis; how is not recorded. Hospital beds and FTE employees per 1,000 (The Dartmouth Atlas of Health Care, n.d.-a) are described but not in the models. Dartmouth codes -88888 and -99999 are set to missing; the original code turned them into positive values (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026, in a working session).

Cause of kidney failure mapping. The original mapping spreadsheet is no longer available. The replacement maps every code of USRDS format $PD96DET (United States Renal Data System 2024), a public list of about 445 ICD-9 and ICD-10 codes with descriptions, to the six categories above. Three cloud large language models, called through ellmer (0.5.0 or later, the first version that sends current Claude models a native structured-output request) (Wickham et al., n.d.) with the same prompt and a structured output schema, each classify every code, and every model’s answers pass the same check: one each from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google. Two models run locally through Ollama, Alibaba’s Qwen and Meta’s Llama, receive the same prompt and are reported for comparison, but do not vote (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 29 October 2026): on the analyst’s laptop neither produced a complete, valid set of answers, and IBM’s Granite, tried first, returned none. A code is accepted when at least two of the three cloud models agree (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 29 October 2026; the rule was three of five while the local models voted, and was chosen because the classification demonstrates the method rather than being the main analysis); codes so accepted are not read by a person, and every other code is reviewed by the deciders, who record the category and their initials (reviewer). Agreement among the models is reported with Fleiss’ kappa in a one-row public summary (references/esrd-cause-agreement.csv), together with the number of codes accepted unanimously, by majority only and not at all; kappa is descriptive and plays no part in accepting codes. A second public file (references/esrd-cause-local-models.csv) reports, for each local model, how many codes it answered, left unanswered or answered with text that is not a code, and how often it agreed with the accepted category. The reviewed mapping is committed to references/ and is the only version the analysis reads. No patient data are given to any model; the local models send nothing over the internet. Patient-level cross-checks against USRDS’s own disease group (DISGRPC, PATIENTS file) and against the published Table 1 counts are run by an authorized person. The prompt defines each category in words, starting from the 12 groups of the 2015 CMS-2728 list of primary causes (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services 2015) and the original spreadsheet’s sheet names: glomerulonephritis includes secondary glomerulonephritis (lupus nephritis, ANCA vasculitis, anti-GBM disease, Henoch-Schönlein purpura) and infection-related glomerular disease (post-infectious glomerulonephritis, HIV-associated nephropathy, hepatitis-associated and cryoglobulinemic glomerulonephritis), while other infections are in Other (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026). HIV-associated nephropathy is thereby counted as glomerulonephritis, unlike in the CMS-2728 grouping, where HIV disease is a miscellaneous condition. The definitions are shown on the website. The mapping written on 30 October 2026 (prompt version 02ff2fbb; 423 codes unanimous, 22 accepted by two of three, none without a majority) is accepted for the replication as drafted, with no changes after review (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 30 October 2026; D4).

7. Outcome

Primary: eGFR at initiation (GFR_EPI, CKD-EPI creatinine equation (Levey et al. 2009) as calculated by USRDS, with the race coefficient) of 10 or above vs below 10 mL/min/1.73 m², as in the original code’s GFR_DICH (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026, in a working session: “Use the code”). The paper describes the outcome as greater than 10 vs at most 10 (Hemmige et al. 2024); the two differ only for patients at exactly 10.

Secondary: eGFR as a continuous variable.

There is no follow-up; the outcome is measured at the index event.

8. Statistical analysis

Descriptive analysis. Table 1 by the five density categories: means (SD) for continuous variables and counts (%) for categorical ones, with classical one-way ANOVA for continuous variables, as the paper says (R’s oneway.test() with var.equal = TRUE; its default is Welch’s test; decided by Vagish Hemmige, 28 October 2026), and chi-squared tests for categorical variables. The paper lists one-way ANOVA, Kruskal-Wallis and chi-squared tests without saying which variable used which (Hemmige et al. 2024); Kruskal-Wallis is not used here. Missing data are reported for each variable.

Primary model. Mixed-effects logistic regression of eGFR ≥ 10 on the density category (reference: 0 facilities), with a random intercept for HSA, progressively adjusted (Hemmige et al. 2024):

  • Model 1: age, sex, race, Hispanic ethnicity, year.
  • Model 2: Model 1 plus heart failure, coronary disease, diabetes, cerebrovascular disease, alcohol or drug dependence, immobility, institutionalized, access type, primary cause of kidney failure, and (in the first version) serum albumin and hemoglobin.
  • Model 3: Model 2 plus insurance, access to nephrology care (none, some, unknown), rural-urban type, ZIP code poverty, ZIP code % Black, and HSA quality benchmarks.

Models 2 and 3 are fit twice, with and without albumin and hemoglobin; with them, the analysis uses complete cases (the paper reports 533,375 patients). The paper used Stata; its exact covariate coding and estimation options are not available, so these models are rebuilt from the paper’s text and Table 2 footnotes. Census region is not a covariate: the original code’s exploratory models adjusted for it, but the footnotes do not list it (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 28 October 2026, to follow the footnotes). The “HSA quality benchmarks” in Model 3 are taken to be the three the Methods give as examples (“such as”): HbA1c testing and eye examinations among enrollees with diabetes, and ambulatory-sensitive discharges. Estimation uses glmmTMB (Brooks et al. 2017) with the Laplace approximation; on synthetic data it matched a fully converged lme4::glmer about thirty times faster, and 7-point quadrature (Stata’s default) changed estimates only in the third decimal (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 27 October 2026). The linear models of eTable 3 also use glmmTMB; the variance decomposition uses lme4::lmer (Bates et al. 2015).

Variance decomposition. Null mixed linear models of continuous eGFR with a random intercept for HSA, and with HSA and dialysis facility (PROVUSRD) nested, report the share of variance at each level. Both models are fit on the same patients, those with a facility ID, so the shares are comparable (decided by Vagish Hemmige, 28 October 2026).

Effect modification. Models stratified by race (White, Black) (Hemmige et al. 2024).

Multiplicity. None; a single primary comparison is reported, as in the paper.

Missing data. Complete-case analysis for each model, plus multiple imputation of albumin and hemoglobin, which the paper describes as “a multiple imputation technique with chained predictive analytics (10 imputations)” (eTable 2), read here as chained equations. Only albumin and hemoglobin are imputed, by predictive mean matching, from the outcome, the density category and the Model 3 covariates, without HSA; estimates are pooled by Rubin’s rules. This imputation model was confirmed by Vagish Hemmige, 28 October 2026 (D7).

Software. R, with usRds (Hemmige 2026), flowchart (Satorra et al. 2026), tidycensus (Walker and Herman, n.d.), glmmTMB (Brooks et al. 2017), lme4 (Bates et al. 2015) and ellmer (Wickham et al., n.d.). The usRds version or commit is recorded with every result.

9. Sensitivity analyses

Analysis Assumption it tests
Exclude HSAs with 0 facilities (reference: category 2); Table 2, Models 1–3 without albumin and hemoglobin That distance to care in HSAs without facilities does not drive the association
Stratified by race, White and Black; eTable 1, Models 1–3 with albumin and hemoglobin That the association holds within each race group
Density as a continuous exposure; eTable 2, Models 1–3 with and without albumin and hemoglobin, with them imputed, and by race That the association does not depend on the choice of cut points
Continuous eGFR as the outcome (linear mixed model); eTable 3, Models 1–3 with albumin and hemoglobin, with and without HSAs with 0 facilities That the association does not depend on the threshold of 10
Multiple imputation of albumin and hemoglobin (eTable 2) That complete-case results are not biased by missing laboratory values
Excluding eGFR of 5 or below (from the original R code; not in the paper) That results are not driven by implausibly low eGFR values
Each correction listed in section 12, applied one at a time How much each correction to the original code changes the published estimates

10. Disclosure control

USRDS data are used under the investigators’ data use agreement. No cell, flowchart box or table entry with a count from 1 to 10 is published, including counts recoverable by subtraction. Tables and figures from USRDS data stay out of the repository until they have been checked for errors and cleared for release.

11. Limitations to address in reporting

The limitations in the paper apply (Hemmige et al. 2024). Specific to the replication: the cause-of-kidney-failure mapping is rebuilt rather than recovered; Stata models are re-estimated in R; and public source files (CMS, Dartmouth, ERS, ACS) are re-downloaded and may have been revised since the original analysis.
